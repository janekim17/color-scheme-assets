CHOOSE A USE, THEN A COMBINATION

Best for campaign announcements, event systems and concise information

Cool pale blue, balanced gray, charcoal, focused red, and burgundy create a decisive palette for announcements and information layouts. Red can lead a poster or mark one action, but its meaning must be deliberate.

Where this palette needs help

In a transactional interface, red may imply an error or destructive action. Do not reuse it for routine success states without a clear system.

02

Choose whether red means identity or a warning

On a campaign poster, red can be the identity field. In a form, reserve warning treatments for actual warnings and pair them with words and icons. A red brand color does not mean every button, status, and heading should share the same visual urgency.

03

One urgent number is stronger than ten red ones

For a briefing page, keep most values in charcoal and highlight the one decision-driving number. For a multi-category chart, do not map both positive and negative outcomes to red and burgundy without explicit labels. Use a neutral comparison series and an annotated focus series instead.

04

Take the selected combination with you.

Copy named roles for a document, identity, or web project. The sheet follows the selected use above; it is not a rule to use all five colors everywhere.

Before extending it, test a real heading, a paragraph, a key value, and a secondary detail. Remove colors without a useful role. For printing, request the output profile and inspect a proof; the approximate CMYK values above are not production specifications.
