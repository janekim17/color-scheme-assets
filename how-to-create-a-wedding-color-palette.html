How to create a wedding color palette across digital and print

An invitation, a details page, and a keepsake can share colors without assigning them the same roles. Start with what each item must communicate.

By Color Scheme Studio · Updated October 20, 2026

The useful starting point

The five soft swatches make useful surfaces and motifs but do not provide a deep reading anchor. Add a functional foreground such as #283D42 where its exact pair passes the required contrast. Keep that addition separate in the handoff: it is not an extra extracted swatch.

Give the invitation a short hierarchy

Names, date, and location need a clear order. A blue-led face can carry a short title and a pale inset for practical details. A longer itinerary belongs on a reading surface rather than being squeezed into small, low-contrast decorative lettering.

Give the website different responsibilities

A details site must support travel information, accessibility notes, and actions. Use a quiet reading field and a consistent action treatment, then design focus and other interactive states. A soft invitation palette is not already a complete website system.

Let the keepsake carry more atmosphere

A sleeve or small label can use terracotta as a broad face or apricot as a large motif. Keep production details on a plain, readable area. The ratio used for the website does not need to transfer to the object.

Check materials without promising exact matches

Paper, fabric, printing process, and lighting affect appearance. Agree on production specifications and compare physical samples when matching matters. A screen HEX code and a generic CMYK conversion cannot guarantee that different materials will look identical.

Record a role sheet for each format

List the chosen field, foreground, panel, action, and decorative colors separately for the invitation and the site. Leave unused swatches in the wider identity library. The goal is a coherent family of useful pieces, not five colors forced onto every item.

Apply the decisions to a real palette

These combinations use the exact values from Spring soft color palette. Change the use, compare the role assignments, then copy a working role sheet. A supporting text neutral is identified wherever one is needed.

Powder blue meets warm clay

Tender Blue introduces a cool note among Warm Beige, Gentle Apricot and Warm Terracotta. Soft White keeps the combination light rather than strongly saturated. In the previews, terracotta takes the larger round shape, balanced by smaller blue and apricot forms; beige stays quiet in the supporting details. The result feels soft, but still has a clear warm centre.
