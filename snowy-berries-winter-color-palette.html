FROM INSPIRATION TO APPLICATION

A quieter take on winter color.

In John Price’s photograph, the snow is a soft gray rather than a brilliant white. Green leaves hold the scene together, and the berries supply small pockets of warmth. Keep that relationship in the design: broad quiet surfaces, a dark green structure, and red used sparingly. This palette suits a seasonal project that should feel thoughtful rather than festive at full volume.

01

Build the season around the quiet colors.

Three seasonal formats, with different amounts of green.

Color balance
Neutral-led: useful when the page contains a large photograph or longer copy. Let the image carry some of the warm color.
Green-led: suitable for a short invitation or a compact cover. Pale text separates from the darker field, while the red motif stays small.
Sage-led: a softer botanical direction for the broad surfaces of a carton. Recheck small type and labels before adapting this mockup.

Original seasonal design studies. Names, layouts, and packaging are illustrative; the carton is not a production dieline.

04

Keep the winter roles together in your CSS.

A light surface, evergreen reading color, and berry-red action for the journal direction.

/* Snowy berries: an sRGB starting palette. Print values need an output profile. */
:root {
  --winter-surface: #D1D2D4;
  --winter-support: #737E67;
  --winter-ink: #33462F;
  --winter-accent: #BB5041;
  --winter-detail: #AD713B;
  --winter-on-accent: #FFFFFF;
}
.winter-card { background: var(--winter-surface); color: var(--winter-ink); }
.winter-action { background: var(--winter-accent); color: var(--winter-on-accent); }

Choose the surface for the message.

For a photo-led journal, begin with the pale version and let the image provide much of the warmth. For a compact invitation, try the green-led version with light text. On a sage carton, use dark lettering and keep small information on a light label. Before printing, confirm the output profile and inspect a proof on the chosen stock; the CMYK values supplied here are approximate conversions.
