CHOOSE A USE, THEN A COMBINATION

Best for independent brands, stationery and editorial cards

Cream, tan, olive, deep olive, and muted pink make a flexible identity palette. The tan and pink have similar visual weight, so the useful decision is often which one to leave out of a particular layout.

Where this palette needs help

Tan and pink are weak category separators without labels. Use dark olive for reading and avoid five equally prominent colored panels.

02

Choose a three-color working subset

For stationery, start with cream, deep olive, and tan. For a short launch card, try pink, cream, and deep olive. Keep the other swatches in the identity library, not necessarily on every object. A smaller working set makes repeatable decisions easier.

03

When two muted colors compete, change their jobs

Tan and pink can become a weak pair of equal data categories. Use one as the surrounding field and the other as a small decorative area, or use a direct label and a darker boundary. Do not make readers infer category names from these colors alone.

04

Take the selected combination with you.

Copy named roles for a document, identity, or web project. The sheet follows the selected use above; it is not a rule to use all five colors everywhere.

Before extending it, test a real heading, a paragraph, a key value, and a secondary detail. Remove colors without a useful role. For printing, request the output profile and inspect a proof; the approximate CMYK values above are not production specifications.
