What is CIELAB? L*, a* and b* explained

Lab describes a colour through lightness and two colour directions. Reading the three coordinates separately makes an unfamiliar specification easier to understand.

By Color Scheme Studio · October 28, 2026

Three coordinates, three different questions.

L* asks how light the colour is. a* locates it along green–red, and b* along blue–yellow. Read the signs as directions, not as amounts of ink.

The three coordinatesRead a comparisonReference conditionsLab and Delta E

What do L*, a* and b* mean?

CIELAB is a colour space introduced by the CIE in 1976. It was designed to describe colour differences more closely in line with perception than raw tristimulus coordinates. It is widely used in colour measurement and colour management. You may see its name shortened to Lab; the asterisks identify the CIE coordinates and help distinguish them from other systems such as Hunter Lab.

For ordinary reflecting surfaces, L* runs from black at 0 to the reference white at 100. Higher L* means greater lightness. It is not the HSL lightness percentage in a design tool: the two models define their coordinates differently.

The other two coordinates work together. A positive a* does not mean “this is a red colour” by itself. If b* is also strongly positive, the result lies toward the red-and-yellow side of the plane. Near a*=0 and b*=0, a colour is close to the neutral axis; its L* then tells you whether it is a light, middle or dark neutral. The a* and b* coordinates are not percentages or a universal −100 to +100 slider.

The sample is lighter, shifted toward green on the a* axis and toward yellow on the b* axis. “Toward green” is relative: a*=17 is still positive, so this is not a statement that the sample has become green. Keeping the sign and the reference in the sentence prevents that common misunderstanding.

This kind of description can help when discussing a print proof or a material sample. “The sample is lighter and more yellow than the reference” gives a clearer direction than “the colour looks wrong.” It does not tell a printer which ink percentage to change; the production process still needs its own controls.

Keep the conditions beside the numbers

Lab is often called device-independent because it does not describe one device’s red, green and blue channels. That does not make its numbers independent of every condition. Calculating CIELAB requires a reference white. In physical colour measurement, the illuminant, standard observer and measurement setup also matter.

Before comparing two reports, check that their conditions agree. If one set uses D50 and another D65, do not treat the coordinates as an ordinary sample-versus-reference difference. Ask for values expressed on a common, appropriate basis. When converting an RGB colour, the RGB colour space or profile must also be known: three unlabelled channel values are insufficient to define the source colour.

A Lab target is also not a guarantee that every printer can reproduce it. The output device, inks and material have a limited gamut. A colour-management workflow translates the target into that output condition, with possible compromises for colours outside its reach.

CIELAB coordinates and Delta E do different jobs

The coordinates locate a colour. Delta E summarises a difference between two colours using a specified formula. The original CIE76 calculation uses straight-line distance in Lab; later formulas, including CIEDE2000, adjust the calculation to better reflect perceptual differences. CIELAB is useful, but it is not perfectly perceptually uniform.

Do not compare a CIE76 result directly with a CIEDE2000 limit. The same pair can produce different numbers under the two formulas. A useful colour specification names the formula and conditions as well as the tolerance. There is no single acceptance threshold that suits every material, colour and viewing situation.

For everyday reading, remember the division: use L*, a* and b* to understand where the difference goes; use an agreed colour-difference method when you need to quantify it. Keep a physical reference and relevant viewing conditions when a real production match matters.
